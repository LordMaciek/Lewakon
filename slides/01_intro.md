# So: a tool to be used wisely.

## What do I propose? A contract.

1. What we must do.
2. What is OK to do.
3. What is forbidden.
* Rules apply to both students and teacher.

---

# We MUST

1. Disclose the use of AI and its extent.
Whenever we use AI to do something associated with the class, we inform about it. Elaborate on what you used it form, to what extent. Try and say why it's a good idea to use it in this case, and what are your concerns. Don't be afraid to do that. Unless you evidently and consciously break the rules, there will be no punishment. It's important that we practise using different tools (AI included) and discuss them. Did it improve the effects of your work? How did it affect learning? Can it be considered dishonest in any context? Finding our way around AI tools is part of what we learn here and I strongly believe that making mistakes is a part of the learning process.

So, when you write the assignments, a part of them will be AI use disclosure. But when you use AI during class to do something – do go ahead and tell us about it. We'll discuss it, share views etc.

2. Use AI with criticism.
That one is pretty self-explanatory. Sometimes AI returns crap. Each and every time we use it to do anything, we must look at the product and evaluate it!

---

# We CAN

## Teacher

1. Use AI to create class materials.
  * generating cool content for presentations (as long as it is cool!)
  * generating data sets for class and homework
  * generating other helping materials, like apps etc.
  * generating early versions of rubrics

2. Use AI to grade homework (Limited!)
  * only use AI to help finding sources relevant to a problem.

3. Use AI to improve the quality of code.
R code evolves, and the way I've learned to do things might be obsolete, and often will be. New functionalities emerge constantly, with new ways of doing things being more convenient, intuitive, and often just better, I want to teach you up-to-date stuff, not some things I've learned 15 years ago.

4. Finding teaching resources, materials, better ways of looking at things.

---

# We CAN

## Students

1. Debug your code.
You wrote your code, it refuses to work. There is a problem which you do not know how to solve. AI is very powerful in terms of coding assistance.

2. Do some editing and spell-checking of your text.
Ask AI to act as a reviewer. NEVER tell it to just rewrite your text. Give it what you've got and ask precise questions. DO NOT use any text generated by AI in your assignments or elsewhere.

3. Discuss problems, look for sources.
AI can be a good teacher, but try not to use this exceedingly. It's not a great teacher. Again, ask precise questions. When it gives you a resource, or a source to cite, you MUST read it and see whether it makes sense.

---

# We MUST NOT

1. Upload class materials or students' work to AI.
Unless it's a very specific fragment. Like code snippet, sentence, or a paragraph you have trouble understanding.

2. Create anything wholesale.
  * Teacher: It's ok to generate and animation for presentation using AI. It's not ok to create a whole presentation using AI.
  * Student: You can't just upload the data to the AI and tell it to write your assignment for you. Or run the whole analysis.

3. Ask AI to tell you what to do step by step.
The analysis you will perform for your home assignments is a series of steps. You cannot ask AI to list those steps for you. You can upload your list and ask whether it's correct or if you are forgetting something.

4. Ask AI to write your code for you.
Knowing how to code in R is one of key learning objectives of this course.

5. Use AI as first choice of help in any problem.
Ask me. Ask a colleague. Go to message boards (stackoverflow!) try and figure out the solution by yourself. The difference between AI as helpful tool and something that makes you stupid can be very thin, but we must strive to stay its right side.
